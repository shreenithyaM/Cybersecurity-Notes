### VPN (Virtual Private Network)
A **VPN** is a service that creates a **secure, encrypted connection** between your device and the Internet.
- It hides your real IP address and encrypts your online traffic.
- Think of it as a **private tunnel** for your data on the public Internet.

**How it works:**
1. Your device connects to a **VPN server**.
2. All your data is **encrypted** and sent through this server.
3. The VPN server forwards your request to the Internet.
4. Websites see the **VPN server’s IP** instead of yours.

**Uses**:
- Access **blocked or geo-restricted websites**
- Protect privacy on public Wi-Fi
- Hide your **real location**
- Secure communication for businesses
- Encryption of data
- Platform independent (works on complete device)

**Example in real world:**
- Using **NordVPN** or **ExpressVPN** to watch content from another country.
- Companies like **TCS** or **Infosys** provide VPNs to employees to securely access company servers.

### Proxy Server
A **Proxy** is an **intermediate server** that forwards your requests to websites.
- It acts like a **middleman** between your device and the Internet.
- Unlike VPN, it **does not always encrypt traffic**.

**How it works:**
1. Your device sends a request to the **proxy server**.
2. The proxy server fetches the content from the Internet.
3. The website sees the **proxy server’s IP** instead of yours.
4. The content is sent back to you via the proxy.

**Uses**:
- Bypass **content restrictions**
- Cache frequently used content for **faster access**
- Hide IP for anonymity in basic browsing
- Platform dependent (works only on 1 browser)

**Example in real world:**
- Using a **school or company proxy** to access certain websites.
- Free proxy websites like **Hide.me Proxy** to browse blocked sites

## Difference between VPN and Proxy
| Feature    | VPN                             | Proxy                                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Encryption | Yes, full encryption            | Usually no encryption                   |
| Security   | High                            | Low to medium                           |
| Speed      | Can be slower due to encryption | Faster (no encryption)                  |
| IP masking | Yes                             | Yes                                     |
| Works for  | Whole device / all apps         | Usually browser only                    |
| Use case   | Privacy, security, geo-blocking | Bypassing content restrictions, caching |


> [!note]
>
> **Why do companies use VPN?**
>
> “Companies use VPNs to provide secure remote access to internal systems and protect sensitive data through encrypted connections, especially for remote employees and branch offices.”

> [!note]
>
> **Is VPN used only to bypass restrictions?**
>
> No. Bypassing restrictions is just one use case.
> In companies, VPNs are mainly used for:
>
>  - secure remote access  
>  - encrypting data  
>  - protecting internal systems  
>  - connecting office networks safely  
>  
> Accessing geo-restricted content is more common in personal use than in enterprise use.

> [!note]
>
> **How does a VPN help remote employees?**
>
> A VPN helps remote employees securely connect to the company’s internal network over the internet by encrypting data and allowing safe access to company resources from anywhere.

> [!note]
>
> **Why is VPN more secure than a proxy?**
>
> A VPN is more secure than a proxy because it encrypts all internet traffic between the user and the network, while a proxy usually only redirects traffic without strong encryption.

> [!note]
>
> **Can companies monitor employees through VPN?**
>
> Yes. When employees use a company VPN, the company can often monitor:
>
>  - websites accessed through the VPN  
>  - network activity  
>  - login times and IP addresses  
>  - data transferred on company systems  
>
> However, monitoring policies depend on the company’s security rules and local privacy laws.
